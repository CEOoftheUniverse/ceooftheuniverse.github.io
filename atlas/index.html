Honest on the photonics example: Australia has real photonic milestones — the 2020 44.2 Tbps single-chip record (Monash · Swinburne · RMIT, in Melbourne, not Hobart) and PsiQuantum's Australian founders. How to read this

Scores are % (higher=better for a move). "Housing cost" = affordability; "Time/$ to relocate" = efficiency from Jeju (Jeju=100 on both — you're already here). Kawaii culture-fit personalises the chart to your taste: Tokyo=100 (Akihabara/Harajuku + birthplace of city-pop/Shibuya-kei); Korea/Jeju partial (cute-cafe + idol culture); Mexico City & Chiang Mai have surprisingly large anime fandoms; Blue-Zone/Alps places score low. Innovation uses a 3–5 yr rolling window so one announcement can't skew it, and scores the local ecosystem in national context — e.g. Switzerland is #1 globally but a Valais alpine town isn't Zurich; Sardinia's Ogliastra is rural (its one claim is longevity genomics); Hobart rides Australia's research but the photonics is in Melbourne. Neither new axis is prediction-market-traded — both are lagging/qualitative, flagged "—". Radiation cleanliness (positive-framed, higher=cleaner) blends the UV burden (latitude · altitude · ozone · surface reflection) with ambient ionizing dose (radon from local geology, cosmic from altitude, terrestrial gamma). It isolates the UV + ionizing sub-signal so you can weight it on its own; it partly overlaps the broad Health column (which also folds in healthcare, air and water), so weighting both double-counts a little — by design, kept separate so the lever is yours. Surprises worth seeing: the Swiss Alps and Sardinia dip (altitude/snow UV + granite/alpine radon), while cloudy high-latitude Reykjavík tops it.
